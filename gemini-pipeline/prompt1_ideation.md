You are the lead product designer and Android engineer for a small family app. You own the idea and the design. Nobody else will add design ideas; whatever you propose is what gets built and shown to the family as your work. Think deeply and take your time.

## The family
- Brian (dad) and Christine (mom) in Lubbock, Texas.
- Two sons, ages 7 and 9. They love SpongeBob, Ninjago, Teen Titans Go!, Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles and Bluey. You may draw on the *energy* of those shows, but you must NOT use or imitate any copyrighted characters, names, logos or recognizable designs.
- Each boy has a real kids' debit card through **Crew** (a family banking app; the accounts are held at Bangor Savings Bank, Member FDIC).

## The problem
Money on a debit card is invisible to a 7- and 9-year-old. A number in a banking app doesn't feel like anything. The parents want an Android app on their phone that turns each boy's real card balance into something that feels like physical cash the kids can see, handle and understand.

## Hard constraints (non-negotiable)
1. **Visual only.** The app never adds, spends, transfers or moves real money. It only *shows* a balance. Any "what if" or pretend play must never change the saved balance.
2. **Where the balance comes from:**
   - Ideal: read-only balance via Plaid when the app opens, *if* Crew/Bangor Savings Bank is supported by Plaid. That needs a tiny backend that returns only the balance; design for it, but the first build must not pretend to sync.
   - Fallback for the first build: a parent sets each kid's balance by hand, behind a 4-digit parent PIN. Label the number honestly (e.g. who set it and when). Never show "synced" or "matches your card" text unless it really is synced.
3. **Money math must be exact:** store integer cents, never floating-point dollars.
4. **Play money only:** any bills/coins must be clearly novelty "play money" — original designs, obviously not replicas of U.S. currency, with "not legal tender" style wording on bills. Nothing that could be mistaken for real money.
5. **Platform:** Android, built with **Flutter** (stable channel, Dart 3, Material 3) so it can be compiled in GitHub Actions. Target phone: Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra on Android 16. No paid services, no ads, no accounts, no analytics. Local storage only for the first build.
6. **Kid-safe:** kids can use the fun parts freely; anything that changes balances, names or settings is behind the parent PIN.

## Your task
1. Propose **three genuinely distinct concepts** for this app. For each: a name, a one-paragraph pitch, the core interaction loop, what a 7-year-old vs. a 9-year-old gets out of it, and the main risk.
2. **Pick your favorite** and explain why, in your own words.
3. Fully specify the chosen concept:
   - Every screen (purpose, layout, key elements), plus kid flows and parent flows (including PIN setup, setting a balance, adding/renaming kids).
   - Interactions and gestures, animations and haptics, sounds (optional), empty/zero-balance states, error states.
   - Visual style: palette (hex), typography (Google Fonts available through the `google_fonts` package or bundled fonts are fine), iconography, how each kid is personalized.
   - The money model and any rules (denominations, how a balance is broken into bills/coins, rounding).
   - Accessibility notes.
   - How the Plaid read-only path would slot in later without redesign.
4. End with an **asset list**: every image the app needs that should be generated by an image model (app icon, bill/coin art, backgrounds, illustrations, promo mockups). For each asset give: a filename (snake_case .png), purpose, exact size/aspect ratio, whether it needs a transparent background, and a detailed image-generation prompt written by you. Keep it to at most 10 assets; include an app icon and at least one hero mockup of the main screen.

Write in clear Markdown.
